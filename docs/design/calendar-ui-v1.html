Calendar panel · five directions

Each panel is the real 640 × 190 pt notch panel, drawn at 1 px = 1 pt in Departure Mono at the app's type sizes (8 / 9 / 11 / 13 / 16 / 24). Every concept shows the same day so they can be compared directly: it is Wed 14 Oct, 11:48, the next event is Design review at 12:00 on Google Meet, and the day runs from a past 09:30 standup to an 18:00 gym session. The red hatching is the camera housing, which nothing below the tab bar may need.

A · Timeline ★ recommended

The rest of today as a horizontal ruler, starting just before now and running into the evening, which suits the panel's wide, short shape. The slice already past is hatched, a white "now" line marks the time, and each event is a coloured block where it actually falls. The next event and its Join button sit above the ruler, and tomorrow fits on one line below it.

Good forSeeing at a glance how full the day is and where the gaps are, without reading anything. It is the layout that makes most use of a panel this wide.
Trade-offShort events become slivers; their titles sit under the bar and run until the next event starts, so two back-to-back 15-minute meetings get cramped. Overlapping meetings need a second lane, which costs a row.

B · Week strip

A seven-day strip across the top, with a dot for each event in its calendar's colour. Click a day to list its events in two columns underneath. It's the iPhone Calendar week header, sized for the notch.

D · Next up

The next meeting is the whole message: a large countdown, the title at headline size, and a solid Join button. Everything after it hangs off a thin rail on the right, ending in a tomorrow divider. It's built for someone who opens the notch to ask "do I need to go now?"
