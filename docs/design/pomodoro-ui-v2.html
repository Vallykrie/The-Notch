Focus timer · five more directions

A second round, after A · Ring, B · Big clock and C · Day strip didn't land (they're kept in pomodoro-ui-v1.html). These lean harder into the app's pixel look and give the timer an object to watch, not just numbers. Same rules as before: the feature is off until it's switched on in Settings → Focus. Every panel is live and shares one clock. Press Start (or Space) and use 60× to watch a whole round. Under each panel is how the closed notch shows it while running.

D · Ripening tomato

A big pixel tomato that ripens as you work: it starts as a pale outline and fills with red, pixel by pixel from the bottom up, until it's whole when the round ends. During a break it's a green tomato filling with green. Finished tomatoes line up on a little shelf: that's your day's count.

Why it might winCharming and on-brand with the pixel mascot. The "harvest" makes finished rounds feel like something you collected. Progress reads without reading any numbers.
Where it falls shortCute can wear thin over months of daily use. Pixel-fill progress is coarse (about 500 pixels), fine for a 25-minute round.

E · Hourglass

A pixel hourglass in the middle of the panel, right under the camera, with sand actually falling: the top drains, a thin stream runs through the neck, the pile grows below. When it's paused, the stream stops. The time sits on the left; the round and controls on the right, so the panel is symmetrical around the notch.

F · Kitchen tape

Like a mechanical kitchen timer: a minute ruler slides under a fixed amber needle. Drag the tape to set any length (when stopped), let go, press Start, and the tape creeps back toward zero. No preset buttons and no settings needed for the focus length; you just pull out as many minutes as you want.

G · Flip clock

Four split-flap cards that flip each time a digit changes, like an old station board or desk clock. Quiet most of the time (the minutes flip once a minute; seconds can be hidden in Settings to make it calmer still). The right side holds the round, the task line and controls.

H · The walk

The whole set of four rounds is a path across the panel, with a flag at each break. A little pixel walker makes its way along it while you focus, sits down by a campfire at each break, and reaches the finish flag after round 4. Where it stands is how far through your session you are.
